# 01 — Product North Star

> Answers the mandate's product questions (§7) and sets the bar for the child
> truth audit (§34) and parent value audit (§35). This is the "why the product
> exists" document that every slice is measured against.

---

## One-sentence north star

**USAM for Kids is an AI-native, child-first learning universe where a
7–15-year-old can enter alone, be understood, and be guided by characters and
adaptive learning through English, coding, AI literacy, and real projects —
producing visible evidence of growth their parents can trust.**

---

## WHO

- **Learners:** ages 7–15, split into three developmental bands.
  - **7–9 (`AGE_8_9`)** — early readers; heavy scaffolding, large tap targets,
    voice-forward, story-driven, minimal text.
  - **10–12 (`AGE_10_11`)** — building independence; more text, real projects,
    early coding and AI concepts.
  - **13–15 (`AGE_12_14`)** — self-directed; deeper projects, real programming,
    AI ethics, entrepreneurship.
  - (Enum values reflect the current backend `AgeBand`; band *labels* are the
    product-facing 7-9/10-12/13-15 grouping.)
- **Parents/guardians:** pay for and monitor; need visible outcomes, safety,
  and progress evidence. Involved by choice, not required for the child to
  operate the product.
- **When the child is alone:** the product must self-orient them — always
  answer "what do I do next?" without a human teacher (mandate §17).

## WHAT (does the child learn)

Core identity domains (deliberate, not "all school subjects" — mandate §12):

1. **English** — full engine: vocabulary → phonics → reading → grammar →
   listening → speaking/pronunciation → writing → conversation (CEFR-aligned).
2. **Coding** — computational thinking → visual/blocks → Python/JS → algorithms
   → debugging → real projects.
3. **AI literacy** — what AI is, ML/LLM concepts, prompting, limitations, bias,
   ethics, responsible use, AI projects (taught, not just used — mandate §15).
4. **Cross-cutting skills** — critical thinking, problem solving, creativity,
   communication, entrepreneurship, digital & financial life skills, surfaced
   through the **Balanced Development** model.

Each maps through the learning hierarchy (mandate §8):
`Domain → Skill → Competency → Concept → Objective → Activity → Practice →
Mission → Project → Assessment → Mastery → Evidence → Recommendation`.

## HOW (learning is delivered)

- **Adaptive:** the adaptive + mastery + difficulty-calibration engines pick
  what's next from real evidence (spaced repetition, ZPD).
- **Story → Learn → Practice → Reward** loop per mission. (The *Learn* teaching
  beat is the current top gap — see gap register.)
- **Mastery-based progression:** advancement is earned by demonstrated mastery,
  not seat time.
- **Remediation + review** driven by the interventions + misconceptions engines.
- **Characters** (Azouz, Zein, Luma, Codey, +) as guides and companions.

## WHY (would they return / would a parent pay)

- Child: a living world that adapts, characters that greet them, visible streaks
  and level-ups, real things they create.
- Parent: measurable outcomes (mastery by domain), a portfolio of real work,
  credentials/badges, safety (COPPA/GDPR consent already live), and a clear
  map of what the child is learning and why.

## WHEN (the journey over time)

- **First session:** language pick → welcome → age band → interests →
  character → a first guided mission with a visible reward.
- **First week:** daily goal cadence, first mastery signals, first recommended
  next steps from real interests.
- **First month:** first project/portfolio artifact, first credential, balanced
  development picture emerging.
- **First level:** demonstrated mastery across a domain's objectives → progression.

## WHAT NEXT (the non-negotiable)

Every screen must answer **"what should the child do next?"** — via the home
recommendations section, mission stepper, and character guidance. Any screen
that leaves the child at a dead end is a defect (mandate §34).

## Child truth audit checklist (§34) — the bar every slice must clear

- [ ] Can a new child understand what to do without a teacher?
- [ ] Can they navigate and recover from confusion?
- [ ] Can they see progress and choose meaningful activities?
- [ ] Can they interact with AI safely?
- [ ] Can they create something and see evidence of growth?
- [ ] Do they always know what comes next?

## Parent value audit checklist (§35)

- [ ] Why would a parent pay? (outcomes visible)
- [ ] What does the child learn / create? (portfolio + mastery)
- [ ] How is progress measured and safety handled?
- [ ] Does pricing map to outcomes? (blocked on packaging model — Phase 3)
